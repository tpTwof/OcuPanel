## Follow-up intervals

Patients with open-angle glaucoma who are stable on therapy should be re-examined
every 3 to 6 months. Visual field testing and optic nerve imaging are repeated every
6 to 12 months. Patients with progressing disease require shorter intervals, typically
every 2 to 4 months.

## First-line therapy

Prostaglandin analogs are the most effective first-line agents for lowering intraocular
pressure and are dosed once daily. Beta-blockers are contraindicated in asthma, chronic
obstructive pulmonary disease, bradycardia, and second- or third-degree heart block.

## Angle status and provocative risk

Anticholinergic agents and cold preparations containing ephedrine may precipitate an
acute angle-closure attack. Risk is low when the angle is open on gonioscopy, but the
history of glaucoma should still be reported before such medication is prescribed.

## Systemic factors

Nocturnal hypotension, obstructive sleep apnea, migraine, and peripheral vasospasm may
impair optic nerve perfusion and should be co-managed with the primary care physician.
